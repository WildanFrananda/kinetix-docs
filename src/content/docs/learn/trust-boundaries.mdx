Every request body is written by someone you do not control. Some of its fields are genuinely the
client's to decide: which product, how many, which delivery tier, how much to top up. Others are
**facts that belong to a service** — what a product costs, what a courier charges, whether money
actually arrived. When a request carries a fact it does not own and the server believes it, the client
decides the outcome.

Kinetix shipped that mistake three times. Each was found by following a number from the request to the
place where money moved, each is fixed, and each fix is running in production. This lesson replays all
three, before and after.

## Nothing was broken, which is the point

In all three cases every service did its job correctly. pricing discounted the base it was given,
escrow held the amount it was asked to hold, the wallet credited what it was told to credit. Each
service's own tests were green while the defect was live. The fault lived **between**
services: a number crossed a trust boundary and nobody asked where it came from.

The fixes are just as uniform, and none of them is validation. A minimum and a maximum cannot tell a
real price from an invented one. Each fix **removed the field** from the request and **asked the
owner** instead:

| Case | Fixed in | What the request lost | Who answers now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit price | [order `ed04256`](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/commit/ed0425605e1e36e12f2126055f38f1e502cf8255) | `UnitPrice`, `ProductTitle`, `CategoryId`, `MerchantPrincipalId` | catalog |
| Shipping fee | [order `1efad89`](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/commit/1efad89e336e9688725fc83edbb13371fab0e0f3) | `BaseShippingFee`, `DistanceKm` | matching and pricing |
| Wallet top-up | [payment `e26b065`](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-payment-service/commit/e26b065dbdc19a3e3c3f3c216e5d9945e78214ff) | the power to credit; the amount became a charge | Midtrans |

## The question to ask of every field

Before a field is accepted, ask: **who owns this fact?** If the answer is not the caller, the field
does not belong in the request.

| Field | Owner | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ProductId`, `Quantity` | the buyer | Accept |
| Price, title, category, merchant of a product | catalog | Remove; ask catalog |
| `ShippingServiceTier` | the buyer | Accept, and refuse a tier that is not offered |
| Shipping fee, distance | matching and pricing | Remove; ask for a quote |
| Top-up amount | the buyer, as the amount to charge | Accept, but credit only what was paid |
| "This payment succeeded" | the payment provider | Verify the signature, then ask the provider |

## Belt and braces

Removing the field closes the hole. Kinetix also adds a second check where the cost of being wrong is
money:

- **Ask again at the moment of charge.** Checkout looks every product up in catalog a second time,
  rather than trusting the price saved in the cart. That second lookup is what protected carts created
  before the fix, which stay in Redis for fourteen days.
- **Cross-check two owners.** pricing's final shipping fee must be a discount of the quote pricing
  itself gave; anything else refuses the checkout before an order exists.
- **Treat a message about money as a prompt, not a fact.** A signed payment notification only tells
  payment to go and look. The credit is decided by Midtrans's status API, and only when its amount
  equals the amount recorded when the charge was made.
- **Record first, credit once.** The top-up is recorded as `PENDING` under an idempotency key before
  Midtrans is called, and credited under a lock on the wallet's owner, so a repeated notification
  cannot credit twice.

## How these were found

Not by a scanner. Each was found by picking a number that ends up in a payment — a price, a fee, a
balance — and tracing it backwards hop by hop to the first place it was set. In all three cases the
first place was a request DTO. The trace is short: the price went from `AddCartItemRequest` to the cart,
to `PriceLine`, to `CalculatePrice`, to the escrow hold — four hops, and none of them asked
catalog. Doing that once for every amount that reaches a payment is a cheap audit, and it is worth
repeating whenever a new field is added to a request.

## Takeaways

- A client may choose. It may not state facts that another service owns.
- Remove such fields rather than validating them; a bound cannot tell true from false.
- Re-resolve the facts at the moment money moves, not only when they first enter.
- For money reported from outside, verify the sender, then ask the source directly.
