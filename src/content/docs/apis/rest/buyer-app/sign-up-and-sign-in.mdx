A buyer gets a session from identity in one of two ways: a new account (step 1), or a sign-in to an existing
one (step 2), followed by a two-factor challenge when the account has it on (step 3). Every account created
here has the role `customer`, which the wallet endpoints require. A buyer app that kept the tokens of an
earlier run checks them with step 4 instead of asking the buyer again. Keeping the session alive and ending
it are on [Stay signed in and sign out](/apis/rest/buyer-app/stay-signed-in/). A buyer app sends no
`X-Kinetix-Client` header, on these calls or on the refresh: identity accepts a request that names no client
([Tokens](/apis/rest/#tokens)).

## 1. Create an account

The buyer types an email and a password. The `201` is a session, the same as a sign-in gives: the app keeps
`accessToken` and `refreshToken`, notes when `expiresIn` seconds will have passed, and keeps two ids from
`user`. `user.id` is the account id that identity's own paths take, such as the
[profile](/apis/rest/buyer-app/profile/) and [two-factor](/apis/rest/buyer-app/two-factor/) endpoints.
`user.principalId` is the `sub` of every token identity issues
([token.service.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4193caf69b2ebea67534e619ebf3a2ccc384e857/src/application/services/token.service.ts#L90-L125)),
the id the other services know the buyer by. A `409` means the email already has an account: sign in with
step 2.

## 2. Sign in

The buyer types the email and password of an existing account. A `200` is one of three answers, told apart by
their fields
([auth_usecase.service.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4193caf69b2ebea67534e619ebf3a2ccc384e857/src/application/services/auth_usecase.service.ts#L48-L68)).
Without `mfaRequired`, the answer is a session: keep it as in step 1. With `mfaRequired: true` there is no
session yet; keep `challengeToken` and its `expiresIn` for step 3. The third, with `mfaEnrolmentRequired`, comes
only to an `admin` account that has not set up two-factor authentication, and opens no session a buyer app
can use.

Two answers make the buyer wait, both `429` with `Retry-After`: one counts the attempts from the client's
address, the other, `SIGN_IN_DELAYED`, counts the checks of this account's password and codes from every
device. After five checks in a row the account's wait grows from 1 second to at most 60, and it never locks
the account
([sign_in_attempt_policy.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4193caf69b2ebea67534e619ebf3a2ccc384e857/src/domain/sign_in_attempt_policy.ts#L3-L35)).
The app shows the wait and lets the buyer try again once `Retry-After` seconds have passed.

## 3. Answer the two-factor challenge

Only after a sign-in that answered `mfaRequired`. The app sends `challengeToken` from step 2 and the six
digits the buyer reads from the authenticator app. A `200` is a session: keep it as in step 1. A challenge
lasts its `expiresIn` seconds and takes at most five codes. Every refusal of the challenge or the code is a
`401` whose `error` tells the app what to do next
([auth_usecase.service.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4193caf69b2ebea67534e619ebf3a2ccc384e857/src/application/services/auth_usecase.service.ts#L86-L137)):

- `WRONG_CODE` leaves the challenge open: the buyer types the code the authenticator shows now, and the app
  sends it with the same `challengeToken`.
- `CHALLENGE_EXHAUSTED` (five codes taken), `CHALLENGE_SPENT` (used up) and `CHALLENGE_INVALID`
  (expired, or no longer answerable) mean the challenge can never succeed, whatever code is sent. The app asks
  for no other code and starts again at step 2, which asks for the password and issues a new challenge.

After a `429` the app waits `Retry-After` seconds, then sends the code again with the same challenge while it
lasts.

## 4. Check a stored session

At a later start, a buyer app that stored the tokens sends the access token here before using it. A `200`
means the token is still accepted, and repeats what the app needs from it: `principalId`, `userId` (the
`user.id` of step 1), `role` and `expiresAt`. When the token has expired, Kong refuses it with a `401`
([Kong's answers](/apis/rest/#kongs-answers)): the app exchanges its refresh token as in
[Stay signed in](/apis/rest/buyer-app/stay-signed-in/), and signs the buyer in again if that is refused too.
