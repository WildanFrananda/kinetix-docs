A buyer gets a session from identity in one of two ways: a new account (step 1), or a sign-in to an existing
one (step 2), followed by a two-factor challenge when the account has it on (step 3). Every account created
here has the role `customer`, which the wallet endpoints require. A buyer app that kept the tokens of an
earlier run checks them with step 4 instead of asking the buyer again. Keeping the session alive and ending
it are on [Stay signed in and sign out](/apis/rest/buyer-app/stay-signed-in/).

## 1. Create an account

The buyer types an email and a password. The `201` is a session, the same as a sign-in gives: the app keeps
`accessToken` and `refreshToken`, notes when `expiresIn` seconds will have passed, and keeps two ids from
`user`. `user.id` is the account id that identity's own paths take, such as the
[profile](/apis/rest/buyer-app/profile/) and [two-factor](/apis/rest/buyer-app/two-factor/) endpoints.
`user.principalId` is the `sub` of every token identity issues
([token.service.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4b8eb5e5f70f05a8a5f9b6bd0d04b2bf88cc8b7f/src/application/services/token.service.ts#L74-L87)),
the id the other services know the buyer by. A `409` means the email already has an account: sign in with
step 2.

## 2. Sign in

The buyer types the email and password of an existing account. Both `200` answers share the status, and the
`mfaRequired` field tells them apart
([auth_usecase.service.ts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-identity-service/blob/4b8eb5e5f70f05a8a5f9b6bd0d04b2bf88cc8b7f/src/application/services/auth_usecase.service.ts#L36-L49)).
Without it, the answer is a session: keep it as in step 1. With `mfaRequired: true` there is no session yet;
keep `challengeToken` and its `expiresIn` for step 3.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.login" />

## 3. Answer the two-factor challenge

Only after a sign-in that answered `mfaRequired`. The app sends `challengeToken` from step 2 and the six
digits the buyer reads from the authenticator app, before the challenge's `expiresIn` seconds have passed.
A `200` is a session: keep it as in step 1. On any other answer the app starts again at step 2, which issues a
new challenge; after a `429`, it waits `Retry-After` seconds first.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.verifyTwoFactor" />

## 4. Check a stored session

At a later start, a buyer app that stored the tokens sends the access token here before using it. A `200`
means the token is still accepted, and repeats what the app needs from it: `principalId`, `userId` (the
`user.id` of step 1), `role` and `expiresAt`. When the token has expired, Kong refuses it with a `401`
([Kong's answers](/apis/rest/#kongs-answers)): the app exchanges its refresh token as in
[Stay signed in](/apis/rest/buyer-app/stay-signed-in/), and signs the buyer in again if that is refused too.
