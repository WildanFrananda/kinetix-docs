A seller's products live in catalog. The seller picks a category, creates the product, reads it as buyers see
it, changes it and withdraws it. Every write asks identity whether the account's merchant may trade, so the
account must have been approved ([Become a seller](/apis/rest/seller-web/become-a-seller/)). The API has no list
of a seller's own products: the product list has no merchant filter and leaves withdrawn products out. The
client therefore keeps the `id` of every product it creates, the only way to change or withdraw the product
later. Stock is not set here; it arrives through the backoffice's
[stock intake](/apis/rest/backoffice/stock-intake/).

## 1. Choose a category

Every product belongs to one category. Send the chosen category's `id` as `category_id` in step 2. Its `slug`
is what a category discount names ([Promotions](/apis/rest/seller-web/promotions/), step 1).

<RestEndpoint id="catalog.listCategories" />

## 2. Create the product

The seller chooses the `sku` and enters the rest; `category_id` comes from step 1. Keep `id` from the `201`.
The create takes no idempotency key: when its answer is lost, reading the product by its SKU (step 3) shows
whether it exists, whose it is (`merchant_principal_id`) and its `id`.

<RestEndpoint id="catalog.createProduct" />

## 3. Read the product as buyers see it

By the `sku` from step 2. `warehouse_stock` reads `out_of_stock` until stock for the SKU has been received
through the backoffice's stock intake.

<RestEndpoint id="catalog.getProduct" />

## 4. Change the product

Send only the fields that change, with the `id` kept from step 2. `is_active` `false` withdraws the product as
step 5 does, and `true` puts a withdrawn product back on sale.

<RestEndpoint id="catalog.updateProduct" />

## 5. Withdraw the product

With the same `id`. The product stays in catalog with its SKU, and step 4 with `is_active` `true` puts it back
on sale.

<RestEndpoint id="catalog.deleteProduct" />
