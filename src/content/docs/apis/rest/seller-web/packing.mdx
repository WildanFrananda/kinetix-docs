Every paid order of a seller's goods becomes a fulfillment task in the warehouse. The seller works the queue:
scan every line of a task, which starts packing it and, at the last line, packs the parcel and tells order; wait
until order has booked a courier and recorded the parcel's tracking number; then print the label. The warehouse
calls need a session whose role is `seller`
([Become a seller](/apis/rest/seller-web/become-a-seller/), step 5). After a `503` `DATABASE_UNAVAILABLE`
([warehouse's answers](/apis/rest/#warehouse)) it is not known whether a change was written: the page waits the
seconds in `Retry-After` and reads the queue (step 4) before it repeats one. The wait is heard on matching's merchant
socket, which the edge carries for the seller web: it tells the page when the courier job of one of the seller's
parcels is booked or changes, so the page knows when a parcel has a courier and its tracking number is on the way.

## 1. A paid order becomes a task

Tasks are not created over REST. Order's checkout saga creates one over gRPC once the buyer's payment is held in
escrow, with the order's number and its lines, each a SKU and a quantity
([CheckoutSagaRunner.cs](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/1395062e2b803ead17d09bc8f50622367c7fd2ab/Application/Checkout/CheckoutSagaRunner.cs#L237-L277)).
Its due time, `same_day_cutoff_at`, follows the merchant's cutoff hour
([Store set-up](/apis/rest/seller-web/store-setup/)).

## 2. Open the merchant socket

The page opens the socket when it loads, before it reads the queue, so that no push about a parcel packed from
then on is missed. It takes the same `seller` session as the warehouse calls: the browser sends the session cookie
and `Origin` with the upgrade by itself, and the page offers the subprotocol `phoenix` or none, never a token. The
edge checks the session as it does for a `/bff/api/` call, so opening the socket counts as a use of the session and
may renew its tokens first; it admits only a `seller`, then opens matching's socket with the session's access token
([merchant_socket.tl](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-edge-service/blob/eb13db007a21ca985ca33eca49e44dacf43e6354/src/http/merchant_socket.tl#L45-L83),
[session_use.tl](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-edge-service/blob/eb13db007a21ca985ca33eca49e44dacf43e6354/src/http/session_use.tl#L22-L70)).
matching verifies the token again and lets the socket join only the channel named after the token's
`principalId`
([merchant_socket.ex](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/7ef67a0ca1d0e8945f638de8a2f9684e84d06886/lib/fleet_pulse_web/channels/merchant_socket.ex#L24-L45),
[merchant_channel.ex](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/7ef67a0ca1d0e8945f638de8a2f9684e84d06886/lib/fleet_pulse_web/channels/merchant_channel.ex#L32-L41)).

<RestSocket id="matching.merchantSocket" />

A browser's `WebSocket` does not show the page the status of a refused handshake, only that the connection failed
and closed. The page then reads the session ([Sign in](/apis/rest/seller-web/sign-in/), step 3):
`authenticated: false` shows the sign-in, a `customer` role is an account not approved yet or whose approval has not
reached the session (Become a seller, step 5), and a `seller` session opens the socket again after a pause.
matching closes its sockets in batches when it restarts
([endpoint.ex](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/7ef67a0ca1d0e8945f638de8a2f9684e84d06886/lib/fleet_pulse_web/endpoint.ex#L19-L26)),
which the page takes as a lost connection: it connects again and joins again (step 3). The Phoenix JavaScript
client does both by itself, connecting again after a short wait that grows to 5 seconds
([socket.js](https://github.com/phoenixframework/phoenix/blob/b5026660dc744424545e7d2e3b7547507443ab40/assets/js/phoenix/socket.js#L182-L188)).

## 3. Join the seller's channel

Every frame is a JSON array `[join_ref, ref, topic, event, payload]`; a reply repeats the `ref` of the frame it
answers, and a push from matching has `null` for `ref`
([v2_json_serializer.ex](https://github.com/phoenixframework/phoenix/blob/b5026660dc744424545e7d2e3b7547507443ab40/lib/phoenix/socket/serializers/v2_json_serializer.ex#L63-L98)).
Join the topic `merchant:` followed by the session's `principalId`, from the sign-in's answer or from
`GET /bff/session` (Sign in, step 3):

<RestSocketMessage id="matching.merchantSocket.join" />

Right after the join, matching pushes the seller's unfinished courier jobs. The page takes the list as the current
state, after the first join and after every reconnect alike, because changes made while the socket was down are
not sent again:

<RestSocketMessage id="matching.merchantSocket.activeOrders" />

The channel only pushes. When it fails, for example because matching's database did not answer while it listed the
jobs, Phoenix pushes `phx_error` and the channel is gone until it is joined again
([socket.ex](https://github.com/phoenixframework/phoenix/blob/b5026660dc744424545e7d2e3b7547507443ab40/lib/phoenix/socket.ex#L560-L569));
the Phoenix JavaScript client joins again by itself, and the new `active_orders` replaces the list. matching closes a
socket that has sent nothing for 60 seconds
([websocket.ex](https://github.com/phoenixframework/phoenix/blob/b5026660dc744424545e7d2e3b7547507443ab40/lib/phoenix/transports/websocket.ex#L26-L35)),
so the page sends a heartbeat every 30 seconds, as the Phoenix JavaScript client does by itself
([socket.js](https://github.com/phoenixframework/phoenix/blob/b5026660dc744424545e7d2e3b7547507443ab40/assets/js/phoenix/socket.js#L174)):

## 4. Read the queue

The queue is the seller's work: tasks still to pack, and packed parcels whose label has not been printed. Keep
each task's `id`, which every later step takes, its `order_number`, by which step 8 matches the socket's pushes, and
its `lines`, which say what goes into the parcel, from which bin, and whether a scan has matched each line yet
(`scanned`).

<RestEndpoint id="warehouse.taskQueue" via="seller-web" />

## 5. Start packing

Moving a `received` task to `packing`, with its `id` from step 4, tells the warehouse the packer has started. The
step can be left out: the first matching scan in step 6 does the same. The same endpoint takes `packed` in step 7.

<RestEndpoint id="warehouse.setTaskStatus" via="seller-web" />

## 6. Scan the items

Send each code the packer scans, with the task's `id`, once for each line. A SKU marks its line as scanned, and the
first match moves a `received` task to `packing`. The scan that matches the last unscanned line packs the parcel
and tells order that it is ready, and order books a courier
([verify_scan_service.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/services/fulfillment/verify_scan_service.rb#L65-L99),
[task_transitions.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/services/fulfillment/task_transitions.rb#L28-L35)).
`lines_remaining` says how many lines are left. The order number or the tracking number matches too but marks no
line, and a scan of a task that is already `packed` changes nothing and tells order nothing. A code that matches
nothing moves nothing.

<RestEndpoint id="warehouse.verifyScan" via="seller-web" />

## 7. Re-send the packed report

A task becomes `packed` only once a scan has matched every line: on a `packing` task, step 5's endpoint refuses
`packed` while a line is still unscanned
([advance_task_service.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/services/fulfillment/advance_task_service.rb#L61-L64)).
Once the task is `packed`, sending `{"status": "packed"}` to that endpoint re-sends the report to order, and this
is what it is for: when the scan that packed the parcel answered `order_notified` `false` with order unreachable,
no courier has been booked yet
([advance_task_service.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/services/fulfillment/advance_task_service.rb#L95-L118)).
Order keeps one courier booking per order, so a report sent again books no second courier
([FulfillmentPackedHandler.cs](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/1395062e2b803ead17d09bc8f50622367c7fd2ab/Application/Fulfillment/FulfillmentPackedHandler.cs#L62-L81)).

## 8. Wait for the tracking number

Order asks matching for a courier for the parcel. matching books the job at once, which the socket pushes as
`pending`, but answers order with the parcel's tracking number (AWB) only once a courier is assigned to it
([courier_telemetry_server.ex](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/7ef67a0ca1d0e8945f638de8a2f9684e84d06886/lib/fleet_pulse/servers/courier_telemetry_server.ex#L24-L61));
until then order asks again later
([CourierDispatcher.cs](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/1395062e2b803ead17d09bc8f50622367c7fd2ab/Application/Fulfillment/CourierDispatcher.cs#L73-L109)).
With the answer, order records the tracking number in the warehouse over gRPC
([CourierDispatcher.cs](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/1395062e2b803ead17d09bc8f50622367c7fd2ab/Application/Fulfillment/CourierDispatcher.cs#L121-L146),
[fulfillment_task_service_handler.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/rpc/fulfillment_task_service_handler.rb#L80-L111)),
and from then on the task's card in the queue carries it as `tracking_number`
([task_queue_service.rb](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-warehouse-service/blob/07fc2707413de029830bd1364e722a41b8285d81/app/services/fulfillment/task_queue_service.rb#L54)).

The page matches each push to a task by `order_number`. A `pending` job has no courier yet: the page keeps waiting.
An `assigned` job has one, and the page reads the queue again (step 4). Order learns the tracking number only from
matching's answer to one of its requests, which can come a while after the push, so while that task's
`tracking_number` is still `null`, the page reads the queue again later. Once set, `tracking_number` equals the
job's `awb_number`; no label can be printed before. Later pushes, `picked_up` and `delivered`, follow the parcel
on its way to the buyer.

<RestSocketMessage id="matching.merchantSocket.orderUpdated" />

## 9. Print the label

With the task's `id`, once its `tracking_number` is set. `awb_number` is the tracking number the label carries.
After the first print the task leaves the queue; the seller finds it again by its order number
([Returns](/apis/rest/seller-web/returns/), step 1).
