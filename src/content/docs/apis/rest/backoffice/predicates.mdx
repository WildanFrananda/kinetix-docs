Seal issues each courier a credential of their own deliveries, rating and days active, and anyone can check one: a
lender or an employer learns only whether the courier meets a predicate, never the numbers. Only an admin decides which
predicates exist, so a verifier cannot narrow a courier's numbers down with thresholds of its own. The admin, signed in
from Kinetix's staff network as on [Sign in](/apis/rest/backoffice/sign-in/), lists the predicates, reads one, approves
new ones and withdraws old ones. Seal serves the two reads to anyone and checks the token itself on the two writes,
where it must be an admin's.

## 1. List the predicates

Every live predicate comes back with its rows. A predicate becomes one row in the credential registry per week: the
row's fourth minimum is the week's first issue day the predicate's freshness window allows, and seal keeps this week's
row and last week's. `placed` turns true once the registry holds the row.

<RestEndpoint id="seal.listPredicates" via="backoffice" />

## 2. Read one predicate

Read a predicate by the `id` from step 1 or step 3, for example to see whether its rows are placed yet, or what a
withdrawn one asked for.

<RestEndpoint id="seal.getPredicate" via="backoffice" />

## 3. Approve a predicate

Send the class, the label verifiers will see, the three minimums and the freshness window. The answer is the new
predicate with its rows, not yet placed: seal writes them to the registry on its next pass, within about ten minutes,
paying from its own sponsor wallet.

<RestEndpoint id="seal.approvePredicate" via="backoffice" />

## 4. Withdraw a predicate

Withdrawing stops new presentations under the predicate's rows once seal has taken them out of the registry, again
within about ten minutes. What was proven under them before stays readable, and approving the same minimums later makes
the same rows again.
