The backoffice is the web application the API expects Kinetix staff to use. It is for accounts whose `role` is
`admin`: the staff endpoints on the following pages answer `403` to any other role. A staff member signs in with an
email and a password, answers a two-factor challenge when the account has two-factor authentication on, and the
application then keeps the token pair fresh until the staff member signs out. Being a browser application, the
backoffice is subject to CORS and authenticates with the bearer token alone, never a cookie; see
[Browsers](/apis/rest/#browsers).

## 1. Sign in with email and password

The email and the password the staff member types go to identity in one request. A `200` carrying `accessToken`
starts the session: keep `accessToken`, `refreshToken` and `expiresIn`, and read `user.role`, since an account whose
role is not `admin` signs in here but is refused by every staff endpoint. A `200` carrying `mfaRequired` has started
no session yet: keep `challengeToken` and go to step 2.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.login" />

## 2. Answer the two-factor challenge

Only an account with two-factor authentication on gets here. Any account, an admin's included, turns it on with the
calls on the buyer app's [Two-factor authentication](/apis/rest/buyer-app/two-factor/) page. Send `challengeToken`
from step 1 and the six digits the staff member's authenticator shows, within the challenge's `expiresIn` seconds. A
`200` is the same token pair as step 1's, kept the same way; a `401` sends the staff member back to step 1.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.verifyTwoFactor" />

## 3. Restore the session from a stored token

When the application loads again with a stored access token, this call says whose token it is, with what `role`, and
when it expires (`expiresAt`), without asking for the password. Its fields come from the token itself. A `401`, from
Kong or from identity, means the access token can no longer be used: step 4 exchanges the refresh token for a new
pair.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.me" />

## 4. Refresh the token pair

Once `expiresIn` seconds have passed, or after a `401` for an expired access token, exchange `refreshToken` for a
new pair and replace both stored tokens at once. The refresh token sent is spent, and sending it a second time ends
the session, so a backoffice open in several browser tabs must let only one of them refresh at a time and share the
new pair with the others. Any `401` here means signing in again at step 1.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.refresh" />

## 5. Sign out

Send the access token in `Authorization` and the session's `refreshToken` in the body; leave `refreshToken` out to
end every session of the account instead. Discard both stored tokens after the `204`.

<RestEndpoint id="identity.logout" />
