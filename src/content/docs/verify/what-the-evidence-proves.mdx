An order's history, its delivery and its reviews live in the databases of the services that own them:
order, matching and review. When a buyer disputes a delivery, or a merchant says a review was changed, the
only record is in those databases, and nobody outside Kinetix can read them. The evidence anchoring makes
those records checkable later, without trusting Kinetix to report on itself.

## Each owner keeps an append-only log

Each log is written by the same transaction as the change it records, and Postgres enforces it:

- **Order** keeps every status an order reaches and every step of its return, with who made it, in
  `order_lifecycle_events`. A status with no event of its own in the same transaction cannot commit, and the
  log refuses `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE`
  ([the triggers](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/ebcaa31d2ec221774902f054a5f34456bced9f31/Migrations/20261016134224_OrdersKeepALifecycleLog.cs#L62-L127)).
- **Matching** keeps every delivery with the SHA-256 of its photo and of its signature, in `delivery_evidence`.
  A job cannot become delivered without its row in the same transaction
  ([the log](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/cfea287f14b21d36639afc9e196aec13387f3b16/priv/repo/migrations/20261016160000_deliveries_keep_an_evidence_log.exs#L9-L79)).
  The hash is of the bytes that were stored: the courier's upload URL is signed over the photo's SHA-256 and
  refuses a second write to the same name
  ([the upload](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/cfea287f14b21d36639afc9e196aec13387f3b16/lib/fleet_pulse/storage/minio.ex#L21-L29)),
  and matching checks the stored object's SHA-256 against it before it records the delivery
  ([the check](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-matching-service/blob/cfea287f14b21d36639afc9e196aec13387f3b16/lib/fleet_pulse/dispatch.ex#L276-L295)).
- **Review** keeps every product review and courier rating as it was written, the comment as the SHA-256 of
  its UTF-8 bytes, in `review_evidence`. A trigger writes the row in the statement that inserts the review;
  a row written any other way, and any change to one, is refused
  ([the log](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-review-service/blob/d259ba53f8ce03651eec94f8fe4e1faf1544c6b2/database/migrations/2026_10_16_000001_reviews_keep_an_evidence_log.php#L18-L116)).

These rules hold for every code path, but not for the database's owner, who can drop a trigger. What holds
against the owner is the anchor.

## What the notary commits to

The notary reads each log through its owner's feed, from a cursor of a transaction id and a sequence. A
feed serves only rows whose transaction is older than every transaction still running, so a record whose
transaction commits late is served late, never skipped
([order's feed](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-order-service/blob/ebcaa31d2ec221774902f054a5f34456bced9f31/Infrastructure/Persistence/OrderLifecycleFeed.cs#L10-L30)).

Every record gets a random 32-byte salt of its own
([the salts](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/internal/entropy/salts.go#L17-L29)).
Its leaf is the Keccak-256 hash of the stream, the salt and the digest of the record's fields
([the leaf](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/internal/keccak/evidence.go#L30-L37),
[the record](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/internal/keccak/evidence.go#L68-L120)).
A day after a stream's last batch closed, or as soon as 65,536 records are waiting, the records collected
since then go into a Merkle tree
([when a batch closes](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/internal/evidencing/closer.go#L120-L123)).
The batch is anchored in the evidence registry on Base Sepolia: its root, its number of leaves, the feed
position it reads through, the time it closed, and the digest of the batch before it
([the digest](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/internal/keccak/evidence.go#L52-L66)).

The registry takes each stream's batches in order and refuses any that does not follow
([`anchor`](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/contracts/src/evidence/EvidenceRegistry.sol#L78-L125),
[the checks](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/contracts/src/evidence/EvidenceRegistry.sol#L255-L284)):

- only the anchorer's key may anchor, and only the stream's next batch, after the head it names;
- a batch is never empty, closes after the batch before it and not in the future, and reads through a feed
  position strictly after the one before;
- the same batch sent twice is refused as already anchored, and a different batch with the same number as a
  conflict.

So consecutive batches show that each log was read in order, without a gap, and an anchored batch cannot be
changed, dropped or reordered. Nothing on chain names a buyer, an order or a review: a leaf is a hash over a
salt that only the notary and the record's parties hold, so a guess at a record cannot be checked against
the chain.

## What a proof shows

A proof gives one record, its salt and the sibling hashes on the path from its leaf to the root. When it
verifies against an anchored, final batch, it shows that:

- the record, exactly as the proof states it, was in its owner's log when the batch closed;
- it has not changed since, because the batch's digest is chained into every batch after it;
- for a delivery, the photo and the signature whose SHA-256 it carries existed then, byte for byte; for a
  review, so did the comment.

The verifier computes the path itself and asks the registry contract to compute it too
([`verifyInclusion`](https://github.com/WildanFrananda/kinetix-notary-service/blob/b5d5e1138dcde472ee90c48c8ed3fc315e9c8a2c/contracts/src/evidence/EvidenceRegistry.sol#L156-L175)).
It gives a verdict only when both agree.

## What it does not show

- **That the record is true.** The log says what the owner wrote. A courier who reports a delivery that did
  not happen is recorded as having delivered. The evidence fixes what was claimed and when, which is where a
  dispute starts.
- **What happened before the batch closed.** Until then only Postgres guards a record, and the database's
  owner can drop a trigger. A day's records are anchored at the end of that day.
- **That every record was included.** The registry knows where each batch reads through, not what the log
  held. A record left out shows when one of its parties asks for its proof and is never given one.
- **That the proof stays private.** The proof carries the record in the clear, and checking it through an RPC
  endpoint hands that endpoint the record's salt and digest.

To check a proof of your own, see [Verify the evidence of an order or a review](/verify/evidence/).
